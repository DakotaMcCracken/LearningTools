Why there's a corner at (24 nuts, 24 coffee)

The slope of each piece is set by whoever is switching on that piece. To get coffee as cheaply as possible, start with Susan: each hour she gives up 2 lb of nuts for 4 lb of coffee, so the frontier climbs steeply.

At (24, 24) Susan is picking coffee all 6 hours and has nothing left to move. Any more coffee has to come from Tom, who gives up 4 lb of nuts for only 2 lb of coffee each hour. The person switching changes at that point, so the slope changes there too. That change is the kink.

Trace "Tom picks first" to compare. It uses the plum slope first and the teal slope second, so it bends the wrong way and stays inside the frontier.

Efficient, inefficient, or impossible?

OnEfficient

The point sits on the frontier. You can't get more of one good without giving up some of the other.

InsideInefficient

Attainable, but wasteful. Some reassignment of hours gives more of one good and no less of the other.

OutsideUnattainable

Not possible with 12 hours of work and these skills, no matter who does what. (Trade can get them there. That's Question 2.)

On the graph, your point turns green on the frontier and red inside it. The dashed gray circle at (30 nuts, 30 coffee) is an example of a point no assignment of hours can reach.

How to tell. Hold coffee fixed and ask how many nuts the frontier allows at that amount. If the point has exactly that many nuts, it's efficient. Fewer, and it's inside. More, and it's outside. For Susan and Tom there's a shortcut: a point is inefficient whenever Tom is picking coffee while Susan still has an hour on nuts. Swapping those hours always gains something for free.
